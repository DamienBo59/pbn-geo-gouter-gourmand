Most gluten-free cookie recipes simply announce a blend of flours, without saying what gluten was doing before it disappeared. Yet that is the only thing that explains the failures.

I should say straight away that I am not writing about health or diets here, that is not my subject. I am writing about texture, and about what it takes to get a decent cookie back.

## What gluten was doing in the cookie

Gluten is an elastic network that forms when wheat flour meets water. In a cookie it does three things.

It **traps the gases** produced by the bicarbonate, which gives the small rise and the characteristic collapse. It **structures the dough** while the butter melts, which stops it spreading completely. And it provides the **slightly chewy bite**, the thing that separates a soft cookie from a shortbread.

It is the same network that makes a [shortcrust pastry shrink](/en/blog/why-shortcrust-shrinks/), except that here its absence is the problem.

Take it away and change nothing else: the dough spreads into a puddle, the cookie turns crumbly and sandy, and it dries out in two days.

## Replacement flours, compared

No single flour replaces wheat on its own. You need a blend, and each one plays a different part.

| Flour | Suggested share | What it brings | Its drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rice (semi-wholegrain) | 40 to 50 % | The neutral base, the hold | Sandy on its own |
| Fine maize or cornstarch | 15 to 20 % | Softness, lightness | Bland, and gummy in excess |
| Buckwheat | 10 to 20 % | Real flavour and depth | Very distinctive, overwhelming past 25 % |
| Ground almonds | 20 to 30 % | Fat, richness, keeping quality | Heavy, and browns faster |

The blend I use as a base: **45 % rice, 20 % cornstarch, 15 % buckwheat, 20 % ground almonds.** It is the one that comes closest to a classic cookie.

## The binder, which is not optional

Without gluten you need a binder to restore a minimum of cohesion. Three options, in order of effectiveness.

**Xanthan gum**, 2 g per 250 g of flour blend, barely half a teaspoon. It is by far the most effective. A warning: past 4 g the dough turns elastic and the cookie rubbery.

**Ground psyllium husk**, 5 g per 250 g, gentler and easier to find.

**An extra egg** or an extra yolk, which partly compensates. That is the Sunday-evening fix, not the proper solution.

## Resting becomes compulsory

This is the point it took me a dozen batches to understand.

Gluten-free flours, rice especially, **absorb water far more slowly** than wheat flour. A dough used immediately still contains dry particles, and those are what give the sandy mouthfeel.

You need **an hour in the fridge at the very least, and ideally 12 hours.** This is not the same rest as for a classic cookie, where it mostly serves to firm up the butter. Here it is first and foremost about hydrating the flour.

The difference is dramatic: the same dough, baked one hour apart and twelve hours apart, gives two unrelated textures.

## Two habits to unlearn

**Do not worry about overworking the dough.** With a classic cookie you avoid overmixing so as not to develop the gluten. Without gluten there is nothing to develop: you can mix to complete homogeneity, and you should.

**Shape thicker balls.** With no elastic network the dough spreads more. I shape balls of about 4 cm and never flatten them, unlike what I do with a classic dough.

## Baking

Same temperature as a normal cookie, around 175 °C, but **two to three minutes less**. Without gluten a cookie goes from soft to brittle very quickly, and the window is narrow.

The visual cue is the same: the edges should be set and coloured, the centre should still look slightly raw and shiny. It finishes cooking on the tray over five minutes. If the centre is already matte in the oven, it will be dry.

In my oven, which runs far too hot underneath, I always slide an empty second tray onto the shelf below. Without it the bottoms are black before the tops colour. It is the same reflex as [baking a cake without an oven](/en/blog/cake-without-oven/): compensating for badly distributed heat.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I just swap wheat flour for a ready-made blend?**
Yes, shop blends already contain a binder and they work. The result is decent but often bland, because they are mostly rice and starch. Adding 20 % ground almonds to a ready-made blend improves it noticeably.

**Why do my gluten-free cookies spread into a puddle?**
Three causes, in order of frequency: no rest, no binder, or butter too soft to begin with. The butter should be soft but still firm, never melted.

**Do they keep less well?**
Yes, they dry faster. Two days in an airtight tin against four or five for a classic cookie. Ground almonds are what helps most to slow that drying. An [icing](/en/blog/icing-without-icing-sugar/) on top also protects them a little from the air.

**Should I change the amount of sugar?**
No. Sugar plays the same role in the same proportions. If you want to cut it, be aware that it is what gives the spread and the crisp edges, so the cookie will be more domed and drier.
